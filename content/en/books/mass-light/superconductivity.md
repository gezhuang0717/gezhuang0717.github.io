## Pairs, current and magnetic response

In conventional BCS theory, electrons form Cooper pairs and a coherent condensate. Zero resistance and magnetic screening are distinct observable properties. The exponential field profile assumes a planar surface and the local London limit. The quoted gap relation applies to weak-coupling conventional superconductors; it is not a universal formula for every material.


## 11.1 What superconductivity is not

Zero DC resistance in a superconductor is not explained by “ordinary resistance disappearing at low temperature plus photons becoming massive so radiation stops.” A conventional BCS superconductor forms a coherent condensate of Cooper pairs and develops an energy gap. Electromagnetic response is reorganized, producing persistent current and the Meissner effect. [Theory of Superconductivity (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.108.1175)

For weak-coupling BCS theory,

{{< reader-math "block" >}}\Delta(0)\approx1.764\,k_BT_c.{{< /reader-math >}}

This is a model result for conventional weak-coupling superconductors, not a universal equality for every superconductor.

## 11.2 London penetration depth

The magnetic field inside a simple superconductor decays approximately as

{{< reader-math "block" >}}B(x)=B_0e^{-x/\lambda_L},{{< /reader-math >}}

where {{< reader-math "inline" >}}\lambda_L{{< /reader-math >}} is the London penetration depth. A Proca-like equation for the electromagnetic response can be written heuristically as

{{< reader-math "block" >}}\nabla^2\mathbf B=\frac{\mathbf B}{\lambda_L^2}.{{< /reader-math >}}

This mathematical resemblance to a massive gauge field motivated Anderson’s insight that a gauge field interacting with a condensate can acquire an effective gap without destroying gauge consistency. [Plasmons, Gauge Invariance, and Mass (1963)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.130.439)

## 11.3 Why the analogy to the Higgs mechanism is profound but limited

The condensed-matter Anderson mechanism and relativistic Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism share the central idea that gauge-field excitations can become gapped through coupling to an ordered medium/field. But a laboratory superconductor is a material with a preferred rest frame, finite density, lattice, and electromagnetic environment; the Standard Model vacuum is not literally a superconductor made of Cooper pairs. Analogies are valuable when their limits are stated.
