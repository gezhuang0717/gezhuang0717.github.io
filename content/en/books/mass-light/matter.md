An atom contains a nucleus and electrons. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, collectively called nucleons. A proton is a composite quantum state with valence quark content **uud**; a neutron has **udd**. Neither picture is a list of three isolated little balls: gluons and quark–antiquark dynamics also contribute.

Atomic sizes are typically around 10⁻¹⁰ m; nuclear sizes are around 10⁻¹⁵–10⁻¹⁴ m. These are characteristic scales, not sharp boundaries. The electron is consistent with being elementary within experimental limits.

The Standard Model organizes six quark flavours, six lepton flavours, gauge bosons and the Higgs boson. Quarks and leptons have spin ½; the photon and the other gauge bosons have spin 1; the Higgs has spin 0. A familiar chart has 17 boxes, but this is a display convention: colour, antiparticles, charge states and quantum states make a single universal particle count misleading.

The three generations repeat gauge-charge patterns; they are not discovery dates. Gravity is not part of the Standard Model. Neutrino oscillations require physics beyond its minimal massless-neutrino form.

**A useful question:** which objects in this hierarchy are composite? Atoms, nuclei and nucleons are; electrons and quarks are treated as elementary in the Standard Model. Elementary does not mean that every property has already been explained.
